# GapQuest mode: Career Fair Follow-Up

Read this when the student's request matches this mode (see the Modes table in SKILL.md). Commands run from the GapQuest folder. The Replies and asking-first rules in SKILL.md apply here too.

Most students meet five recruiters, mean to email all of them, and email none. The best time is within 24 hours,
while the recruiter still remembers the conversation. This Skill gets every follow-up drafted in one go, keeps track
of who replied, and nudges the student when a lead goes quiet.

Cubbie's built-in `email-to-professor` writes to professors; this mode handles many recruiter contacts at once, keeps a
contact list across days, and schedules follow-ups.

## When to use

- "Just got out of the career fair, here are my notes" (followed by text, a voice memo, or photos)
- [photo of three business cards] "follow up with these"
- "Who haven't I followed up with from the Deloitte info session?"
- "Did the Amazon recruiter ever reply?"

Do not use it for applying to jobs, cold outreach to people the student has never met, or emails to professors.

## Steps

1. **Collect the contacts.** Read everything the student sent: typed notes, voice memos (transcribe them), photos of
   business cards or name badges, and LinkedIn screenshots. For each person, pull out: name, company, role, email
   (only if it is written somewhere the student sent), the event and date, and the one or two specific things they
   talked about. If the event name or date is missing, ask once for both.

2. **Confirm the list before writing anything.** Text a short numbered list, one line per person
   ("1. Priya Shah, Intuit, campus recruiter: talked about the SWE intern role + your RAG project").
   Ask the student to fix anything wrong and to fill in a specific detail for anyone whose line is vague. Never
   invent a detail of the conversation; a generic follow-up is weaker but an invented one is worse.

3. **Learn the student's basics once.** Use what Cubbie already remembers about the student: name, school, major,
   graduation term, the roles they want, and a resume link or file. Ask only for what is missing, one question at a
   time, and remember the answers for next time.

4. **Draft one email per person.** Each one:
   - Subject naming the event and something specific ("Great talking at ASU's Fall Career Fair: SWE internship").
   - 4 to 6 sentences: thanks, the specific thing they discussed, one line on why the student fits, one clear and
     small ask (a referral to the posting, the right person to talk to, or a 15-minute call), and a sign-off.
   - Mention the resume as attached or linked only if the student has given one.
   - Sound like a student, not a template: plain words, no "I hope this email finds you well".
   Also draft a LinkedIn connection note for each person, under 300 characters, for when there is no email.

5. **Ask before saving.** Text: "Drafted 4 emails and 4 LinkedIn notes. Save the emails as Gmail drafts?" Only after
   a yes, save each email as a draft with Cubbie's Google ability. Never send. If Google is not connected, send the
   drafts as one document (or as separate short texts if there are three or fewer) so the student can copy them,
   and offer to connect Google for next time. Do not put recruiter emails into any other app.

6. **Remember the contacts.** Save each contact to Cubbie's memory as a networking contact: name, company, role,
   email, event, date, the topic, the date a follow-up was drafted, and status "drafted". When the student says they
   sent one, change it to "sent" with the date.

7. **Offer the nudge.** "Want me to check in a week from now on anyone who hasn't replied? Mon 10 am?" Only after a
   yes, create a one-time check-in with Cubbie's reminders ability, worded so it makes sense on its own:
   "GapQuest follow-up check for <event>: search the student's email for replies from <names / domains>; for each
   contact with no reply, offer a short second follow-up draft; update their status in memory."

8. **When the student asks about status** ("who haven't I followed up with?", "did Intuit reply?"), read the saved
   contacts, search the student's email for replies from each contact's address or company domain, and answer with
   one line per person: replied, waiting (days since sent), or not sent yet.

## Replies

Short, plain text, no Markdown, a few lines per message. Lead with the count and the next action. Put each draft in
its own message or in the document, never all in one wall of text. Ask one question at a time and suggest a default
the student can accept with "yes".

## When something fails

- No names can be read from a photo or memo: say which item was unreadable and ask the student to type the name and
  company.
- No email address for someone: give the LinkedIn note for that person and say the email needs an address.
- Gmail draft fails: send the draft text instead and say the save didn't work; don't retry more than once.
- Email search not available: ask the student whether each person replied, and update memory from their answer.
